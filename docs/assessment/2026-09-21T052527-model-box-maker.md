## What was built

The input was one paragraph from the person: a script that makes a printable box for any 3D model he wants to post, with the model dropping in from above, a lip and no hinge, a lid that cannot damage the model, sides that may be a mesh, a little space for flex, a floor matching the model's underside, and the orientation decided for him. The output is a command-line tool, installable with pipx, that reads STL, OBJ, 3MF or PLY, chooses a pose, computes the cavity as the model's drop path on an outward-rounding grid, builds a base and a lid with manifold3d, cuts the outside back to a wall of bounded thickness in plan, checks that both bodies print without support and that the box will stand, and writes two STL or 3MF files the way up they print, a JSON report and an optional preview. A person can now box any model in one command; the graph of 71 items is the specification the code is tested against, one test function per test item, in the repository's own build.

## Verdict

Cost: 71 items, eighteen minutes to write and about forty to read; 2.4 active hours of AI work and 2.2 million tokens out over a 3.6-hour window, of which the graph's own authoring, amendment and status walking were perhaps a fifth; five ratification rounds; 13 items amended after ratification, all of them the AI's doing (five test wordings it got wrong, eight items moved by the change of mind). Returned: a tool whose every behaviour the person had read before it existed, built to that reading in forty-three minutes; a change of mind made as a diff to signed text, read item by item, rather than as a rewrite argued in chat; twelve moved signatures, none silent; a build that refuses an unsound graph or a failing suite; and a record of the AI's own mistaken promises, which nothing else would have kept. Judgement: the graph earned its keep as a hand-off protocol, buying forty minutes of reading for a build that could not drift from what was agreed; it did not make the AI's tests true, and five of thirty-five were signed wrong.
